How it works

Three steps, and only one of them needs your screen.

  1. 1

    Snap

    Photograph the plant or animal. The photo is resized on your device before it's sent.

  2. 2

    Identify

    A vision-language model studies it and writes a field-guide card: name, lifespan, features, range and fun facts.

  3. 3

    Go outside

    Learn what you're looking at in seconds, then get back to the trail. The screen should be the shortest part of your day.

Built on open source

Powered by an open-weight vision model

Swap the model

The app talks to any OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Point it at a bigger or more specialised open model without changing the app.

Keep photos private

Run the model on your own machine and your photos never leave it.

No lock-in, no cost per scan

Open weights mean you choose where it runs and what it costs, from a laptop to a cloud host.

Run it yourself

A tiny Node app with one dependency. Install Ollama, pull a vision model and start the server.

ollama pull qwen2.5vl:7b
npm install
npm run dev

Safety & privacy

Be curious, be careful, and keep your photos your own.

Explore responsibly

  • Never eat a wild plant or mushroom based on an app. Some look-alikes are deadly.
  • Look, don't touch. Some plants irritate skin and some animals bite or sting.
  • Keep your distance from wildlife, and leave nests, flowers and rocks as you found them.

Your privacy

  • No account, no cookies, no tracking, and no database.
  • Photos are resized in your browser, which also removes embedded location data.
  • The app doesn't save photos or results. If the host uses a hosted model, the photo goes to that provider.

Questions

How accurate is it?

Good on common, well-photographed species and weaker on look-alikes, partial shots and poor light. When it isn't sure it says so and lists other possibilities. Treat it as a starting point, not a verdict.

What can't it identify?

Blurry or distant photos, shots with several subjects, and anything that isn't a plant, animal or fungus. It can't tell individual animals apart or diagnose plant diseases.

Does it need an internet connection?

Not if you host it yourself. The model runs on your own computer, so the AI needs no internet. The hosted version needs a connection, and a phone in the field still needs a way to reach the server.
