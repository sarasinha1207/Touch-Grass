How it works

Three steps, and only one of them needs your screen.

  1. 1

    Snap

    Photograph the plant or animal. The photo is resized on your device before it's sent.

  2. 2

    Identify

    A vision-language model studies it and writes a field-guide card: name, lifespan, features, range and fun facts.

  3. 3

    Go outside

    Learn what you're looking at in seconds, then get back to the trail. The screen should be the shortest part of your day.

Why open source?

Powered by an open-weight vision model

Swap the model

The app talks to any OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Point it at a bigger or more specialised open model without changing a line of the app.

Keep photos private

Run the model on your own machine and your photos never leave it. Nothing is saved on the server either way.

No lock-in, no cost per scan

Open weights mean you choose where it runs, what it costs and how it behaves, from a laptop with no internet to a cloud host.

Questions

How accurate is it?

Good on common, well-photographed species and weaker on look-alikes, partial shots and poor light. When it isn't sure it says so and lists other possibilities. Treat it as a starting point, not a verdict.

Is it safe to eat or touch what it identifies?

No. Never eat, touch or approach a wild plant, mushroom or animal based on this app. Confirm with a local expert or field guide first.

Do you keep my photos?

No. Photos are resized in your browser, sent once to the model, and then discarded. There is no database and no account.

Can I run it myself?

Yes. It is a small Node app. Run it with Ollama on your laptop, or deploy it and point it at a hosted open-weight model. The README has the steps.
